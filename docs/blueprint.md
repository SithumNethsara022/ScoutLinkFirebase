# **App Name**: ScoutLink

## Core Features:

- User & Role Management: Secure login, user profiles with comprehensive personal details (including home address, phone number, and 'extra positions' like IT Committee Chairman) that can be fully edited by the user. Scout leaders can view all details of their scouts. Implements a role-based access control system to define permissions for various actions across the application.
- Badge & Award Tracker: Allows scouts and leaders to log proficiency badges and awards. Users must enter the passing date for both proficiency badges and awards. Awards are clearly listed with their passing dates in growing order (Membership, Scout, Chief Commissioner's, Prime Minister's, President's Scout Award) and are linked to a single scout profile. The edit profile tab allows users to modify their badge work status, with dedicated sections to view proficiency badges and awards.
- Award Prerequisite Advisor: An AI-powered tool that, when marking an award (excluding Membership) as passed, guides the user (scout leader) on the necessary proficiency badges and their completion dates. This tool ensures compliance with specific requirements: Junior/Senior Happy Home + two non-public service/camp craft badges for Scout Award; First Aid & Missioner/Public Health & Ambulance + one from practical science/camp craft/explorer/better world framework for Chief Commissioner's Award; one from education/culture/Senior Saver/Better World Framework (excluding Scout of the World/Messenger of Peace) + one from sports + one from farmer + Civics + Venture for Prime Minister's Award; Senior Happy Home + Ambulance (repass) + Quarter Master/Camp Warden + Senior Organizer + one from public service for President's Scout Award.
- Dynamic Authority Chart: An interactive organizational chart accessible from the profile page. It displays the troop hierarchy: GSL, MIC, Scout Leader, ASLs, Instructors (including STL, ASTL, Badge Secretary, Assistant Badge Secretary), Senior Scouts (some as Troop Leaders), JTL, AJTL, and then divides into four sub-troops, each with a Sub Troop Leader, Assistant, and Patrol Leaders. Profiles are linked, allowing users to view roles, contact information (except for GSL and MIC), and filter profiles by sub-troop, patrol, and grade.
- Event Calendar & Participation Logging: A comprehensive event calendar allowing scouts to log their participation in a dedicated 'Events' section within their profile. When logging an event, users are prompted for event name (with suggestions from the calendar), date, place (1st, 2nd, 3rd, participation), and event type (troop, district, national, international, district committee/position, national committee/position). Special rules apply: 'Cantlay Challenge Shield' allows up to 5th place and registers patrol participation; 'Job Week' automatically sets participation to 'above minimum'. Logged participations are sent to senior and higher roles (excluding the Scout Leader) for confirmation.
- Points System & Leaderboards: Automated calculation of individual, patrol, and sub-troop points based on various activities: Awards (Membership: 10, Scout: 15, Chief Commissioner's: 20, Prime Minister's: 30, President's: 50), Proficiency Badges (5 each), Attendance (Daily: 1, 100% monthly/4 consecutive days: +1 bonus), Discipline Issues (negative points set by Scout Leader). Event points vary by type and place (District: 1st-12, 2nd-10, 3rd-8, Participation-4; Troop: 1st-7, 2nd-6, 3rd-5, Participation-3; Cantlay: 1st-15, 2nd-13, 3rd-11, 4th-9, 5th-8, Participation-4; National participation: 5; International participation: 10; District committee/position: 5; National committee/position: 7; Job Week: 1st-10, 2nd-8, 3rd-6, Above Minimum-3). Monthly patrol ratings by senior scouts/higher roles (out of 10 for programs, document maintenance, discipline) contribute, alongside patrol member count (4 members = 4 points) and average individual points. Sub-troop points are the average of patrol points. Notifications are sent for patrol rating requests. Best scout, patrol, and sub-troop of the month/year are recognized. A dynamic leaderboard on the home screen allows users to switch between individual, patrol-wise, and sub-troop-wise views.
- Role-Based Edit & Undo Controls: Empowers appropriate user roles (original uploader and higher-ranking roles) to edit or delete various entries. This includes: calendar events, discipline issues, attendance records, badge work entries, messages (deleted for everyone after sending), meeting cancellations, announcements, committee dismissals, and reports. This feature ensures flexibility and administrative control over data accuracy.

## Style Guidelines:

- Primary color: A deep, forest green (`#2E473D`), reflecting the natural and traditional aspects of scouting, used for headers and key informational blocks.
- Secondary background color: A slightly lighter, muted green (`#1F3229`), providing a dark, contrasting backdrop for content cards and enhancing readability of lighter text and icons.
- Accent color: A vibrant gold/yellow (`#D4AF37`), used for welcoming text, important highlights, and interactive elements to provide visual warmth and emphasis, aligning with 'gold troop' branding.
- Icon colors: A diverse palette of vibrant colors (e.g., green for attendance, blue for programs, red for discipline) for icons to differentiate features and add visual appeal against the dark backgrounds.
- Headlines and body text font: 'Inter', a clean, modern grotesque sans-serif font. It ensures high legibility across all application elements, from titles to detailed data, maintaining a professional and contemporary feel.
- Utilize clear, outline-based icons with distinct colors to represent different features and actions. The icons should be simple, easily recognizable, and consistent in style across the application to ensure intuitive navigation.
- Employ a structured, grid-based layout for dashboards and content presentation, allowing for organized display of various data points and features. Cards are used to group related information, ensuring responsiveness and a clean, modular appearance across different screen sizes.
- Incorporate subtle, non-intrusive animations for state changes, transitions between screens, and feedback on user interactions (e.g., button presses, data loading). These animations should be quick and smooth to enhance the user experience without causing distractions.
